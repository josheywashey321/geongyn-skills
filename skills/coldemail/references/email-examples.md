# Approved email examples

Five real, Josh-approved cold emails from a single prospecting session, across different business types. Study the **rhythm and moves**, not the exact words: every one opens with a specific observation that proves real research, backed by a hard fact; names the villain (a competitor or the dated status quo) without trash-talking; reframes the lead's own strength as the reason to act; matches the brand's register; runs ~150–220 words; and closes on the free-audit ask. Signature is always `Josh / Geongyn — geongyn.com`.

Each example notes the **angle bucket** it came from (see SKILL.md "Pick the angle").

## 1. Youth gymnastics club — angle: *shows up but undersold* (redesign warranted, dated GoDaddy site)

Later refocused on **defending an existing #1 position via a website redesign**. Voice: warm, respectful of a young award-winning owner.

**Subject:** Spectrum's #1 spot in AI is sitting on a GoDaddy page

> Hi Briana,
>
> I asked AI what a Simcoe parent would — *"best gymnastics for kids in Simcoe"* — and Spectrum comes up first. Great position to be in.
>
> Here's the thing: that position is more fragile than it looks. AI increasingly decides who to recommend by *reading a business's website* — and right now yours is a GoDaddy builder page with the details that matter (schedules, programs, your Club of the Year story) locked inside PDFs and a third-party flip-book that AI mostly can't read. Your #1 spot is resting on a foundation a competitor could knock you off of.
>
> A modern rebuild handles both halves of that. It gives AI clean, structured pages — real program pages, your inclusion story, reviews, an FAQ — that *hold* your top spot as competitors catch up. And it gives parents a site that actually converts: on-page registration instead of downloads, and a clear "Sign Up" above the fold.
>
> Want me to run a free audit through my system at Geongyn to show you exactly where the gaps are?
>
> Josh
> Geongyn — geongyn.com

**Why it works:** opens with the exact query a parent types + the real result (first). Turns their strength (being #1) into a risk (loss-aversion). Every criticism is concrete (GoDaddy, PDFs, flip-book). The "second gen is already modernizing" type of detail proves real reading.

## Cross-cutting patterns worth copying

- **The opener is always the AI-query finding**, stated as a small story ("I asked AI what a parent/patient/guy would…"). It's the proof-of-work that separates these from spam.
- **One hard number early** (review count, years, ranking) makes the compliment unfakeable.
- **Exactly one villain** — a named competitor, or the dated/leaky status quo — never a pile of them.
- **The close is identical in spirit every time**: a free audit, "no pressure, just the rundown." Low commitment is the point.
- **Register flexes to the brand** while the structure stays constant.
