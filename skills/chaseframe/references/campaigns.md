# Campaigns, funnels and marketing plans

## FATE as the funnel

At campaign scale the four rungs stop being sections and become **stages**. A funnel is a ladder the buyer climbs over weeks, and each stage has one job.

| Stage | Rung | Job | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | **Focus** | Break the script; get noticed by the right segment | Cold outbound, paid social, category content, PR |
| Consideration | **Authority** | Become the source they credit | Technical content, methodology, spec data, comparisons, the honest limitation |
| Preference | **Tribe** | "The people I'm like already chose them" | Case studies, named references, community, regional proof |
| Conversion | **Emotion** | Convert agreement into motion | Cost-of-inaction framing, the true cautionary case, the after-state |
| Retention | **Tribe again** | Keep them inside the group | Ongoing reporting, community, account reviews |

**The diagnostic use is the valuable one.** When a funnel underperforms, find the missing rung before rewriting assets:

| Symptom | Broken rung | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody engages at all | **Focus** | The outreach is category-conventional. Nothing deviates. |
| Traffic, no depth | **Focus → Authority** | You got attention and gave no reason to credit you |
| Long engagement, no shortlist | **Tribe** | They believe you and think you serve someone else |
| Shortlisted, then stalls forever | **Emotion** | Full agreement, no forcing reason to move now |
| Closes, then churns | **Tribe (retention)** | They never joined a group, they bought a service |

A stalled pipeline is nearly always a missing Emotion rung, and nearly always gets treated as a pricing problem. Discounting a stall does not create motion; it removes the last reason to value the thing.

## Sequencing a cold outbound campaign

The ladder across touches. Each touch does one rung — attempting all four in touch one is what makes cold email read as cold email.

| Touch | Rung | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Focus | One specific, verifiable observation about *their* business that proves you looked. No pitch. |
| 2 | Authority | How you know it. The method, the finding, a number they can check. |
| 3 | Tribe | Someone like them — their size, their region, their trade — and what changed. |
| 4 | Emotion | The concrete cost of the current situation, in a scene. |
| 5 | Ask | One small yes, low friction, easy no. |

**Never open at rung four.** Cold outbound that opens on fear ("you're invisible to AI and losing customers") is asking for an emotional response from someone who has not granted attention, credited the source, or seen anyone like themselves. It reads as a threat from a stranger, which is exactly what it is.

## Building a marketing plan on the ladder

For a full plan, run the ladder as the plan's own structure.

**1. Segment commitment.** One primary segment. Its dominant need, paired fear, and decision filter, written down. A plan serving three segments equally is three underfunded plans.

**2. Rung audit of what exists.** Score current assets against the four rungs. Most established businesses are heavy on Authority (they have specs, credentials, history), thin on Tribe, and empty on Emotion. The gap tells you where the budget goes, and it is rarely where the instinct says.

**3. Channel assignment by rung.** Channels are not equally good at every rung:

| Channel | Strong at | Weak at |
|---|---|---|
| Cold outbound | Focus | Tribe (no ambient social proof) |
| Paid social | Focus, Tribe | Authority (low-trust context) |
| Search / AI answers | Authority | Emotion (the reader is in evaluation mode) |
| Technical content | Authority | Focus (nobody arrives on it cold) |
| Case studies | Tribe, Emotion | Focus |
| Community / events | Tribe | Scale |
| Referral | Tribe, Authority at once | Volume, control |

Referral is the only channel that delivers Tribe and Authority simultaneously, which is why it converts several times better than anything else and why it cannot be scaled on demand.

**4. Sequence, don't parallelize.** Running all four rungs at once splits budget across stages the audience has not reached. Fix Focus first — if nobody is entering, improving the case studies changes nothing.

**5. Measure by rung.** Each rung has its own honest metric:

- Focus → reply rate, scroll depth past the fold, click-through
- Authority → time on technical pages, doc downloads, depth per session
- Tribe → case study views, referral mentions, "who else do you work with"
- Emotion → proposal-to-close rate, stall rate, cycle length
- Action → conversion on the small yes

A funnel measured only at the last rung cannot tell you which of the first four is broken, which is how teams end up rewriting a working CTA for a year.

## Content series as identity building

Hughes' mass-persuasion sequence is **Idea → Ideology → Identity**, sealed by public declaration (C; E), and his belief formula is **Focus, Emotion, Agitation, Repetition** (D-DOAC-1 1:44). For a content programme:

- **Repeat one true idea across many posts.** Repetition is the mechanism, not a weakness. Vary the angle, keep the idea.
- **Name one identity and keep naming it** ("owners who check before their competitors do"). Over weeks the audience starts to hold it; the CTA then confirms an identity instead of asking for a decision.
- **Make the ask self-directed.** A comment keyword or DM is the buyer asking — the strongest rung (B-1Ua).

## The feed cycle as a retargeting sequence

He describes feeds as FATE at speed: a novelty clip, an authority figure, a tribe signal, an emotional clip, then an ad (D-DOAC-2 1:00; D-MW 0:09). Map a retargeting sequence onto the same order:

| Touch | Rung | Asset |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Focus | The organic Reel that earned the view |
| 2 | Authority | The method shown — a live check, a teardown clip |
| 3 | Tribe | Owners like them — anonymous, true |
| 4 | Emotion → ask | The cost of waiting, then the offer with full expectancy (what happens next) |

## Advertising's two jobs

"Make you feel like you're not enough *yet*" and make you compare (C-SRS 1:08). The honest version is comparison against a real benchmark, with "yet" keeping it closable. Shame without a path burns the audience.

## Community

His "good cult" build for retention (E): an identity statement, insider language, a private assessment, unexpected bonuses, peer milestones, meetups, a shared ritual, an initiation letter. Legitimate when the identity is real and leaving is easy. See [identity.md](identity.md).

## Where urgency is legitimate

Emotion is the rung most likely to tempt fabrication. The line:

**Legitimate** — a real deadline, genuine capacity limits, a true seasonal window, an actual ongoing cost, a price change that is really happening, a competitor genuinely gaining ground.

**Not** — countdown timers that reset, "2 spots left" when there are not, invented scarcity, fake recent-purchase notifications, statistics with no source.

The honest version is almost always stronger, because it survives the question "how do you know?" The manufactured version dies the first time someone reloads the page, and takes the rest of the campaign's credibility with it. This is Geongyn's verified-claims rule and it applies to every asset built with this skill.

**Identity beats scarcity** (B-1Ua): "this isn't for every shop", backed by a real qualifier, pulls harder than a timer and never collapses under a refresh.

The strongest legitimate urgency is nearly always **the compounding cost of the status quo** — it is true by default, it is specific to them, and it does not expire.
