# Short-form video and video ads

Reels, Shorts, TikTok, Meta video ads, and the first 60 seconds of a VSL. Built from Hughes' 90-second capture map (A-B8L), the feed sequence (D-DOAC-2 1:00; D-MW 0:09), the six-axis model (A-uWq) and his identity work. Source codes are in [evidence.md](evidence.md).

**The organizing fact:** a feed is FATE run at speed — novelty, then an authority figure, then a tribe signal, then emotion, then an ad (D). A Reel that wants action runs the same four beats inside one video, and adds **expectancy**: the viewer knows exactly what happens if they act.

## Before writing a line

Commit to these six, in writing, above the script:

1. **Need** (one primary from the 2018 map) and its **fear**.
2. **Decision style** (one of six) — this decides how the CTA justifies itself.
3. **Identity line** — who they want to be, pitched about 10% ahead of how they see themselves now (A-B8L). "The owner who checks before the competitor does", not "a successful owner".
4. **The one uncomfortable truth** — true, specific, slightly unwelcome. If you can't write it, the video has no emotional rung.
5. **The two Legos** — two true facts that, side by side, imply the conclusion without you saying it (D-DOAC-2 1:04).
6. **What happens after the CTA** — the literal steps. This fills the expectancy axis.

## The beat map (30–60 seconds)

Compressed from his 90-second map (0–10 authority imprint, 10–30 novelty break, 30–60 identity, 60–90 uncomfortable truth). The order is the point; skipping a beat makes the next one "exponentially more difficult" (A-B8L).

| Time | Beat | What goes here | Axis filled |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | **Novelty + authority in one window** | A calm declarative statement that breaks the viewer's expectation, delivered by someone who looks like they need nothing. Authority cue inside this window, not later (D-DJ 1:00) | Focus |
| 2–10s | **Name the category, boring and early** | "This is a risk problem, not a marketing problem." First name wins (A-PYf) | Focus → Openness |
| ~8–12s | **CTA #1, inside the open loop** | Said once, early, with expectancy: what they comment, what happens next | Expectancy |
| 10–25s | **Identity, dissociated** | Three types of people / "the owners who…" — never aimed at "you". Then the two Legos, unconnected | Connection, Suggestibility |
| 25–40s | **Uncomfortable truth + stakes** | The cost of the status quo, shown not said. Comparison with "yet" | Emotion |
| last 8s | **CTA #2 as identity, not scarcity** | "This is for owners who…", then the literal steps. Pitch drops on the command | Compliance, Expectancy |

Fill **at least three** of the six axes (Focus, Openness, Connection, Suggestibility, Compliance, Expectancy). Expectancy is the cheapest and the most often empty.

## Hook patterns (true claims only)

Every hook is a **statement first**. A bare question in the first line reads as need and invites "no" (A-VD1).

| Pattern | Shape | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Script-break statement** | "Some of your no-heat calls never touch Google." | Deviation from the expected opener (E-KC 0:36) |
| **Category reframe** | "This isn't a website problem. It's a who-gets-named problem." | First name wins; boring category locks the frame (A-PYf) |
| **Three types** | "There are three kinds of [trade] companies in AI answers right now." | Self-sorting into the good type (E-MN 0:08) |
| **Comparison, yet** | "It names two [trade]s in your town. Not you. Yet." | Advertising's two goals, gap kept closable (C-SRS 1:08) |
| **Dissociated observation** | "Most owners who get named never asked to be." | Observation about the world, not about the viewer (A-B8L 12:24) |
| **Concrete novel image** | an unfamiliar, specific picture rather than a stock metaphor | Common metaphors are inert (E) |

## Mid-video tools

- **Two Legos.** Fact A, then Fact B, then stop. Never say "which means…". "Any idea that you think came from your own mind, you have no ability to resist it" (D-DOAC-2 1:04). Both facts and the implied conclusion must be true.
- **Dissociation.** Aim persuasive lines at "a certain kind of owner" or "most people", never "you". He says the unconscious doesn't filter by pronoun (A-B8L 12:24). Negative dissociation — a true observation about people the viewer wants to step away from — is the strongest form (D-DOAC-2 0:22).
- **Resonance over pitch.** Name what they're already carrying ("you've had the month where the trucks were full and the phone went quiet") before they do. Speak to who they are, not what they need (E).
- **"I imagine" over certainty.** "I'd imagine you've…" can't be refuted; a flat claim about the viewer can (E).
- **Don't over-explain.** The person who explains the most has the least power (A). One reason, said once.
- **Open loops.** Open one early ("watch who it names") and pay it off late. Don't close the identity loop for them — let them finish it (A-B8L).

## The CTA block

Use the compressed **Socialize → Minimize → Rationalize → Project** order (C-SRS 1:35), true statements only:

> *Socialize* "The owners who check this first are the ones who get named."
> *Minimize* "Comment SCAN. Two quick taps."
> *Rationalize* "Most owners haven't checked because nobody shows them this."
> *Project + expectancy* "If it fits, you pick a time for a free 30-minute teardown."

**Identity beats scarcity** (B-1Ua). "This isn't for every shop" does the work of a countdown timer honestly — when a real qualifier exists, as it does in a DM qualifier. Never invent a deadline or a "2 spots left".

**Always offer the private route** in the same breath ("Comment SCAN, or DM me SCAN") — platform policy and lower friction.

## Video-ad skeleton (the Infomercial Cascade)

From his own teardown of an infomercial (B-UQW 11:32): **capture focus → lead focus → status payoff → self-visualization → real constraint.**

1. Hook (novelty + authority in one window).
2. Results arriving on screen — the real process, shown.
3. The status payoff — what peers see when it's fixed.
4. "Even [least-likely person] can do this" — the viewer pictures themselves acting.
5. A **real** constraint. No real constraint? Use the compounding cost of waiting.

## Longer formats (VSL, webinar, 3+ minute video)

**Fractionation, true version:** alternate stakes and relief 2–3 times before the ask — cost of the problem, what fixed looks like, cost, fixed (D-DOAC-1 1:51). Where exactly to place the ask in the cycle is contested in his own telling; place it after the final "fixed" beat.

## Delivery notes for the person on camera

- **Look like you need nothing.** The single biggest authority cue (A-B8L 02:08). No "Hey guys!", no eager smile in the first seconds. An eye-smile later is fine (A-RbQ).
- **Slower than feels natural.** Stillness reads as composure; rushing reads as need (A; E).
- **Pitch drops on the command line.** Tone carries the embedded command when spoken.
- Scripts are a placebo without congruence (E). Film only lines you'd say off camera.

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Segment, decision style, identity line, uncomfortable truth, two Legos, expectancy — all written down
- [ ] Line 1 is a statement, and the authority cue is in the first window
- [ ] Category named early and boring
- [ ] Persuasive lines dissociated, not aimed at "you"
- [ ] At least three of the six axes filled; Expectancy filled
- [ ] Every claim true and checkable; no Hughes statistic; no invented scarcity
- [ ] **Ethics test:** if the viewer could see inside your head — your intent, your reasons — would they still agree to this? (E-DRVN 2:20)

## Worked example — HVAC, ~45 seconds

Segment: Significance / fear of being passed over unseen / Investment. Identity line: the owner who checks before the competitor does.

> **HOOK:** Some of your no-heat calls never touch Google. *(still, unsmiling, calm)*
> **JOSH:** I run AI-visibility checks for service companies. Watch who it names. *(authority in the first window; opens the loop)*
> **VISUAL:** live, logged-out ChatGPT query — "furnace stopped, who should I call tonight in [town]" — names covered with solid blocks
> **CTA:** Comment SCAN, or DM me SCAN — two taps and I'll tell you if a teardown makes sense.
> **JOSH:** There are three kinds of HVAC companies in these answers. Invisible. Named, but described wrong. And the one it recommends with a reason.
> **JOSH:** *(Lego A, from the real query)* It named two shops. *(Lego B, from the same query)* Neither one has more reviews than the companies it left out. *(stop — don't connect them)*
> **JOSH:** Nobody calls to tell you they went with the other shop. The phone just doesn't ring.
> **JOSH:** The shops it names aren't better. They're easier for it to explain. Yet.
> **CTA:** This isn't for every shop — it's for owners already doing real volume. Comment SCAN, or DM me SCAN. Two quick taps. If it fits, you pick a time.

Fills Focus (hook), Expectancy (both CTAs spell out the steps), Connection (three types, resonance), Emotion (the silent phone). Lego B is only filmed if the live query makes it true on the day.
