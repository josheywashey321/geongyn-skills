Chase Hughes' frameworks in plain terms, with provenance attached. Rebuilt 2026-10-10 from 46 primary videos (32 on his own channel, 14 long-form podcast appearances) and his own written resources. Source codes resolve in [evidence.md](evidence.md), which also holds the version history, the critiques and the do-not-use list.

| `[primary-written]` | His own site, charts or worksheets |

**Two rules that apply to every entry below**
1. His *frameworks* are stable across a decade of interviews. His *numbers* are not — he misstates studies and admits to inventing illustrative figures. **Never reuse a Hughes statistic.** Cite the original study or nothing.
2. **Who comes before what.** He says it in nearly every interview: "there's no magic words" (JRE-1), scripts are "a placebo" (E-MN), "make your goal being a good communicator, not being good at tactics" (E-MN 0:22). Every framework here amplifies a speaker who is composed, credible and actually perceptive. It cannot replace one.

## FATE — the influence sequence `[primary-spoken]`

**Focus → Authority → Tribe → Emotion.** "The four ways to manipulate or influence a mammal" (DOAC-1).

He states the order is deliberate — "listed in order of importance" — and gives the rung-by-rung failure logic himself (E-KC 0:35:54–0:36:55). He maps it onto advertising unprompted: an infomercial grabs focus, shows authority, shows "a million people who've already bought it", then shows emotion *visually* (DOAC-1). On SRS: "if a commercial can take control of FATE… action every single time."

| Missing rung | What the audience does |
|---|---|
| No Focus | Never enters |
| Focus, no Authority | Reads it, doesn't credit it |
| Focus + Authority, no Tribe | Believes it, decides it's for someone else |
| All but Emotion | Agrees completely, does nothing |

Refinements from the 2026 pass:
- **Emotion means life patterns and anticipation, not generic feeling** (B; C). The emotional rung lands hardest as "who you become" — a new identity, often with a quiet sense of being ahead of others (C, JRE-2 ~2:00).
- **Authority is often conveyed *through* the tribe** — who else trusts this (B).
- **Alignment lives in the top two rungs** (Tribe + Emotion); compliance can be produced by the bottom two alone and dies when you leave the room (A-Gz0 02:37).
- **Psyop signature** (useful as a credibility check on your own work): focus narrowing, authority rising, tribe hardening, emotion overriding thought (B).

## Focus comes from novelty first `[primary-spoken, versioned]`

2017 (FIC — Focus → Interest → Curiosity): "the easiest way to… get someone to start focusing on you is to have authority."
2023–2025, repeatedly: **novelty is the fastest way to manufacture focus**, and people become responsive to authority *after* focus (B-UQW ~10:29; E-PBD; E-KC; C-JRE-1). "Credibility is novelty first."

Practical reading: open with a subtle surprise, and put the first authority cue **inside the same opening window** — not later (D-DJ 1:00). Calibration matters: the novelty must be subtle. Overdone, you become "a weirdo" (A-B8L 09:19). Common metaphors are inert; unfamiliar, concrete images register (E).

## Autopilot and scripts `[primary-spoken]`

The brain runs role scripts to save energy ("our brains run on scripts", E-KC 0:36:55). Scrolling is a script. Step one of influence is a moment of "wow, I didn't expect that" (E-KC). Deviation is read as **threat or value** (C).

Two uses:
- **Break** a script: deviation from the expected opener ("Let's do this a little bit differently", A-RbQ).
- **Borrow** a script: copy the cues of a context people already trust — an audit report, a spec sheet, a safety briefing — and the reader runs that format's "act on this" habit (B, *script borrowing*). Authority can be borrowed from familiar formats.

`[contested-science]` His Reticular Activating System anatomy story is not validated as he tells it. Keep the behavior (novelty and threat capture attention); drop the neuro language.

## The six-axis model `[primary-spoken]`

His map of what makes influence work anywhere (A-uWq 01:43–12:46; C-JRE-1):

| Axis | Means |
|---|---|
| Focus | Attention held on you, competing stimuli excluded |
| Openness | Safe to be candid, no fear of social cost |
| Connection | Feels connected; wants future contact |
| Suggestibility | Accepts and acts on a suggestion |
| Compliance | Acts on a direction (distinct from suggestibility) |
| Expectancy | Can predict what comes next and whether it'll be good |

"Only three of these qualities need to be present for somebody to be persuaded" (A-uWq 07:30). Use it as a pre-flight check on any asset: which three does it fill? **Expectancy is the one copy most often leaves empty** — "Book a call" with no description of what the call is.

## Authority — five elements `[primary-spoken, primary-written]`

**Confidence, Discipline, Leadership, Gratitude, Enjoyment.** Confirmed in DOAC-1 (said three times), DOAC-2, MW, DJ, PBD, JRE-1 and his own channel (A-MlL, A-TiF), and in his written authority inventory (F). *An earlier version of this file quoted "dominance… fun"; no source retrieved in this pass says that.* Confidence is defined as **safety** — "willingness to receive social injury" (C). Enjoyment is "the most magnetic human trait" (E).

How authority actually shows:
- **Composure over charisma.** Composure is what grants permission (B). Look like you need nothing from the interaction — the single biggest cue (A-B8L 02:08).
- **The person who explains the most has the least power** (A). "Explanation invites negotiation" (B). Declarative beats justified.
- **Authority is built off camera** — environment, time, appearance, social skills, finances — "who you are first, then what you say" (C). People are "starving for something more real" (C).
- **Authority signals that an action is allowed** — "persuasion = permission" (B).
- **Things that kill it** (A-MlL, F): rushing, over-apologizing, bragging, pity-seeking, knocking competitors, smiling as a tool in the first seconds.

`[contested-science]` **The agentic shift.** Hughes treats Milgram's "agentic state" (responsibility transfers to the authority) as fact. The 2023 engaged-followership research finds compliance depends on identifying with a cause the person believes is worthwhile. For marketing that reading is *more* useful: **authority converts when it's fused with the audience's own goal** — which ties rung 2 to rung 3. Don't claim authority "dissolves objections"; claim it lowers felt risk when the buyer shares the aim.

## The Human Needs Map `[primary-written, versioned]`

Resolved (F): the conflicting counts were dated versions of one official map.
- **2018 onward (current, *Six-Minute X-Ray*):** primary — **Significance, Approval, Acceptance**; secondary — **Intelligence, Pity, Strength/Power**.
- 2015–2017: seven (Appreciation, Acceptance, Approval / Intelligence, Pity, Admiration, Power). "Seventeen" has no primary source — ignore it.
- A profile is **one primary + one secondary need** (his worksheet's PRI/SEC slots). In 2026 he listed five in passing, without Pity (B-qEh) — treat as shorthand, not a new version.
- His own map says it "was not made to be an academic study." Use it as a segmentation heuristic, never as a diagnosis.

| Need | Paired fear | Lever |
|---|---|---|
| Significance | Being dismissed as small | Recognition; treated as consequential |
| Approval | Rejection | Reassurance they're doing it right |
| Acceptance | Being left out | Everyone like them is already here |
| Intelligence | Being fooled | Data, not pitch; let them reach the conclusion |
| Pity | Being disbelieved | Name the difficulty as real before the fix |
| Strength/Power | Looking weak | Control and options; never a submissive position |

**Default lever, then fear.** His default opening is gain-framed affirmation of the two universal needs — "you are important, people like you" (C-SRS). Fear is the reverse side, pulled once the need is known: with an intelligence-need buyer, tell the story of someone who made the unintelligent choice and let them decline to be that person (DOAC-1).

Underneath the fears sits **shame** — survival through exclusion ("I can't afford to fail"). Targeted carefully, it's the strongest identity lever; see the ethics gate in [identity.md](identity.md).

## The Decision Map — six styles `[primary-written]`

How a person justifies a yes (F; Behavior Compass DEC axis):

| Style | The question they're asking | Frame that lands |
|---|---|---|
| **Deviance** | Will this help me stand out or break norms? | The contrarian move most of the category won't make |
| **Novelty** | Is this visibly new, and will others see it? | First-mover, new channel, new capability |
| **Social** | Will people show interest or connect with me for this? | Who notices, who else is in |
| **Conformity** | Are my peers doing this; is it acceptable? | Standard practice, peers already moving |
| **Investment** | Will this return more than it costs? | Lifecycle math, cost of one lost job |
| **Necessity** | What specifically makes this necessary vs the alternatives? | The forcing function, the failure prevented |

**Need tells you what to say; decision style tells you how to justify it.** A claim aimed at the wrong style reads as irrelevant, not false — which is worse.

Values layer (his compass VAL axis): freedom, recognition, connection, growth, experiences, information (B; F).

## PCP — Perception → Context → Permission `[primary-spoken]`

Before persuading, ask: "in what context would the decision be automatic?" Then build that context, set the frame first, collect agreement, and the ask becomes a formality (C-JRE-2; D-DJ). Context is most of persuasion — choose the setting for openness (B).

The frame has four pieces (A-RbQ): **Expectation** (the autopilot script — break it gently), **Belief** (what they assume is true — exceed it), **Perception** (the story they tell about you — what feels true), **Definition** (their role — respect it; it loosens once the first three move). "You are either creating the frame or you're stepping into someone else's" (A-RbQ 00:34).

**Category framing:** name the category first, early, calm, as if obvious. Boring is stronger — "risk", "safety", "maintenance", not "opportunity". The category is a permission package: it decides which behaviors feel reasonable and which feel reckless (A-PYf; C-JRE-2 ~2:00).

## Socialize → Minimize → Rationalize → Project `[primary-spoken]`

His confession protocol, which he maps onto infomercials himself (C-SRS ~1:29–1:35; E-MN 0:46): socialize the product ("people like you already do this"), minimize the size of the step, rationalize the cost or delay, project the blame away from them ("most people don't own this only because nobody told them"). Each step shrinks how big the action feels and how much blame attaches. Order of the middle two varies between tellings; the functions don't.

## Idea → Ideology → Identity `[primary-spoken]`

Mass persuasion targets "I am" statements; public declaration seals them (C; E). Cognitive dissonance is the "wall", identity is the "hallway" (D-DJ 1:06). Identity beats logistics and identity beats scarcity — see [identity.md](identity.md).

## Advertising's two goals `[primary-spoken]`

"Make you feel like you're not enough yet" and make you compare yourself to others (C-SRS ~1:08). Add FATE and you get action. The honest version for our work: **comparison against a real benchmark, with "yet" keeping the gap closable** — never shame.

## FEAR — Focus, Emotion, Agitation, Repetition `[primary-spoken]`

His self-programming and mass-belief formula (D-DOAC-1 1:44; E-DRVN 2:15). For content: repetition of one true idea across many Reels is a feature, not a flaw.

## Fractionation `[primary-spoken; placement contested]`

Cycle emotion up and down; each return goes deeper. Feeds do it, he says, for 4–5 cycles before an ad (D-DOAC-1 1:51). **Where the ask sits in the cycle is contested** — trough in JRE-1, right after the lift in SRS. Use the safe part: alternate true stakes and relief (cost of the problem → what fixed looks like → cost → fixed) in longer formats, then ask.

## The cognitive-virology model `[primary-spoken]`

How an idea spreads person to person: **Attach** (acknowledge something true now), **Enter**, **Replicate**, **Defend**, **Transmit** (B-X30). He maps it to copywriting and sales himself. His rule: **if the listener feels persuaded, it failed.** Ideas spread when people retell them as their own — which is the mechanism behind "make them feel clever" in [techniques.md](techniques.md).

## The Infomercial Cascade `[primary-spoken]`

His teardown of a 2 a.m. fishing-lure purchase at 14 (B-UQW 11:32–13:35): capture focus → lead focus (results arriving on screen) → chemical (imagined status payoff) → behavioral ("anyone can do it" — self-visualization) → resources (a real constraint). ## Profiling tools (for live calls, not copy) `[primary-written]`

- **Social Stability Scale** — locus of control, following behavior, esteem, each 1–3 (F).
- **Behavior Compass** — needs, decision style, values, plus a sensory axis (`[contested-science]`, NLP-derived — ignore it).
- **Behavioral Table of Elements** — 120+ behaviors with "deception ratings". `[contested-science]` — see [evidence.md](evidence.md). Use only the order he gives for reading: **change from their own baseline → clusters → context → likelihood** (E-DRVN 0:53), and never conclude "they're lying".
