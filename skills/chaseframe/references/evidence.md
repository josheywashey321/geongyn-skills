## His numbers — never reuse

He misstates studies and sometimes says so himself ("really I'm making that up", JRE-1 ~0:05). Rogan's team fact-checked a figure live (C). Known misstatements:
- **Asch:** "almost 100%" conformed. Published: about 75% conformed at least once; about 37% across critical trials.
- **Drive-safe sign** (Freedman & Fraser 1966, which he credits to Cialdini): he says ~1% vs 89%; published ~17% vs 76%. The mechanism holds; his numbers don't.
- **Milgram:** pre-experiment predictions and the obedience percentage vary between his episodes (0.4% / 0.8% / 0.08%; "67%"). Canonical baseline: 65% (26/40).
- **Kitty Genovese "35 witnesses":** the original account has been substantially revised.
- **Bernays:** bacon-and-eggs is documented; margarine colour, the food pyramid and others are unverified.
- His own figures — "+1,800% CTR", "$10M script", "67% BMW sales lift", "100% trial win rate", "200% money-back guarantee" — are single-source anecdotes.

## The critiques

The main public critic is **Zach Elwood** ("People Who Read People", Gr-gpeJxG8E, plus written summaries), joined by others (F). The criticism targets his **credentials and claims about himself** and his **lie-reading claims** — not ordinary persuasion:
- Inflated biography and military role; a claimed jury-consulting relationship a known trial consultant had never heard of; a paid promotional feature presented as a magazine cover; pay-to-feature awards; an untraceable testimonial; conflicting figures for his own research spend.
- His system described as largely repackaged NLP.

**The science behind the contested parts:**
- Lie detection from behavior averages about **54% accuracy** — near chance (Bond & DePaulo 2006); behavioral cues are weak (Hartwig & Bond 2011); NLP eye-accessing cues fail (Wiseman 2012); body-language reading in security contexts has been called pseudoscience (Denault 2020).
- Milgram's "agentic state" is contested by the engaged-followership account (2023).

**What even the critics concede:** framing, story, novelty, social proof, calm confident delivery and reframing are standard, well-supported persuasion. That is the part this skill relies on.

## Do not use

| Excluded | Why | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Concluding someone is lying from behavior | ~54% accuracy; reputational and ethical risk | Notice a *change from that person's own baseline* and ask a better question |
| BToE "deception ratings" | Not validated | Same as above |
| NLP sensory systems, eye-accessing cues | Failed testing | Ignore the Behavior Compass sensory axis |
| Reading needs from appearance (pets, tattoos, clothes) | Unvalidated stereotyping | Infer needs from what people say and choose |
| Covert hypnosis, "confusion → command" on unwilling people | Ethics gate; weak evidence | Clarity and composure |
| Cult phases 8, 9, 12 | Manipulative core | See [identity.md](identity.md) |
| Any Hughes statistic | Repeatedly misstated | Cite the original study or nothing |
| Invented scarcity, fake deadlines, unverifiable claims | Verified-claims rule | Identity framing; compounding cost of waiting |

## Attribution in client work

**Never name Hughes, or cite his credentials, as an authority in anything a client or buyer reads.** Given the public credential disputes, invoking him undermines the Authority rung it's meant to build. Internally, call these "heuristics popularized by Chase Hughes", and lean on the independent research where it exists.

The critics' red-flag list doubles as a checklist of what never to put on a page: vague or unverifiable credentials, "secret" knowledge, huge fast promises, pay-to-feature awards, testimonials from untraceable people. Sophisticated buyers run exactly this skepticism.
